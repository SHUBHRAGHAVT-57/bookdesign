The problem

People read, nod and forget. Re-reading and highlighting feel productive but are weak study techniques. Typing tools build speed on random text and never check understanding.

Evidence level: established in the research literature, details to verify (see IMPACT.md).

The solution

A digital book you write yourself. Correct letters turn from faint text into permanent ink. A recall question checks understanding. Echo Review brings back the exact words you struggled with, then returns them later to see if they stuck.

Why it is different

  • Starts from the reading, not flashcards you must write
  • Typing skill and understanding are scored separately
  • Transparent rules, no black box, no AI claims
  • Fully offline; data never leaves the device

Who it helps

  • Students revising dense material
  • Language and vocabulary learners
  • Professionals studying technical text
  • Anyone who struggles to hold on to passive reading

No medical or therapeutic claims.

Impact: hypotheses we can measure

  • Retention: better delayed recall than re-reading the same material.
  • Echo works: weak words come back and are remembered on the first try more often than not.
  • Engagement: learners finish more of a passage set.
  • Honest reporting: fast, accurate typing never claims understanding.

Pilot plan: within-subject, two comparable passage sets, delayed quiz after 2-7 days (see IMPACT.md).

What exists today

  • Working offline app and tests
  • Memory Report with an Echo effect line
  • Page history, copy summary
  • Presenter Demo assist

Roadmap

  • Next: import your own text on-device; cross-session spaced review; CSV export; accessibility themes; language packs.
  • Later: teacher mode (opt-in, privacy first); optional human-reviewed question drafting; a real pilot study.

Known limitations

  • Transcription can be shallow; recall and Echo counter it, untested at scale
  • Keyword-based recall grading
  • Six English demo passages

Prototype status: impact is a hypothesis to test, not a result. No statistics are claimed here.
